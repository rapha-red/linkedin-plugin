# Post patterns

Three layers make a post: how it opens, how the body moves, how it ends. Choose each for what the material
already contains. A pattern is a shape to fill with the user's facts, never a promise of reach: no pattern here
has been tested for reach on LinkedIn (evidence.md G1). IDs point to `../../linkedin/references/evidence.md`.

## Opening moves

Every opening says the point, or puts the reader inside it, within the first line or two (P9).

| Move | What it looks like | Good for | Needs |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Claim** | the position in one plain sentence | an opinion, a lesson, advice | a claim the user would defend, and what holding it costs them (P6) |
| **Scene** | a dated moment: where, who, what happened | a story that carries a lesson (P2) | the point arriving within a few lines |
| **Number** | a figure with what it measures and when | results, costs, build logs | a real number from the user (W4, W5) |
| **Change of mind** | what they believed, and that they stopped | lessons, reflection | the event that changed it, told in the body |
| **Mistake** | what went wrong, stated flat | failure stories (P3) | no framing sentence around it ("I'll be honest...") |
| **Observation** | something specific noticed in the work | field notes, trends seen first-hand | the concrete instance, not the generalisation |
| **Answer** | the direct answer to a question people keep asking the user | advice, how-to (P1) | the question named, so the reader knows if it is theirs |
| **News** | what happened, plainly: launched, hired, left, shipped | announcements | a straight statement, no "humbled to share" (P4) |
| **Reaction** | someone's idea in one line with credit, then the user's step beyond it | commentary on a talk, article, post | an extension or counter of the user's own, not agreement alone |

A question as the first line delays the point unless it is the exact question the post answers; prefer the
**Answer** move.

## Body shapes

| Shape | Beats | Good for | Watch |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Story** | situation, what happened, the turn, what the user does now | lessons from experience | one scene, told in order; the lesson is one line, not a paragraph |
| **Claim and proof** | claim, one or two pieces of evidence from the user's work, the limit or cost, close | opinions, positions | proof from their own work, not "studies show" |
| **Before and after** | how it was, what changed, what it cost, how it is now | a change they made | the cost; without it this reads as an ad |
| **Steps** | a numbered procedure the reader can follow | how-to, checklists worth saving | as many steps as the procedure has, not three by reflex; plain numbers, no emoji bullets |
| **Breakdown** | one thing (a decision, a document, a failure) taken apart piece by piece | teaching through a real case | each piece concrete; stop when the pieces stop teaching |
| **Two options** | two ways of doing one thing, what differed, which the user picks and why | decisions, tool or method choices | one variable at a time; say which they chose |
| **Counter-advice** | the common advice, where it broke for the user, what they do instead | contrarian positions | state what they do; skip the "it's not X, it's Y" frame (voice.md) |
| **Ledger** | line items with numbers and what each measures | costs, time spent, results | every line a real figure |
| **Field note** | one observation, why it matters, one line of implication | short posts, work in progress | stop after the implication |
| **Credit** | who did what, named, and what it made possible | thanks, team wins, departures | tag only the people named (R22) |

## Closes

| Close | Good for |
|---|---|
| **Flat stop**: the last true sentence, then nothing | stories, observations, most posts |
| **Real question**: specific, one the user wants answered (P10) | when the user will read and answer the replies |
| **The reader's next step**: what to do differently tomorrow | advice, steps, counter-advice |
| **Plain ask**: one ask, once (a link, a hire, an intro) | announcements, launches |

A summary of what the post just said is not a close; end one line earlier.

## Reading someone else's post (reuse a structure)

Label it in this order, one line each:

1. Opening move (from the table above), and the exact job its first line does.
2. Body shape and its beats, in order.
3. Close.
4. Devices it leans on: a number, a scene, a named thing, a list, a contrast.
5. Paragraphing and rough length.
6. What the user liked about it (ask).
7. Tells from `../../linkedin/references/voice.md` it uses. A post that did well with them still leaves them
   out of the skeleton.

When it fits no pattern here, describe its beats in plain words instead of forcing a label.

# Team mode: several people posting as themselves

Employee advocacy works when each person shares what they know from their own work, in their own voice: LinkedIn
weighs the author's professional identity and members value knowledge from practitioners (P1, R3). It fails when
a company pushes the same text through many accounts: that is repetitive low-substance content the feed demotes
(R6), AI-assisted texts converge on one generic style (H10), and agreed-in-advance likes and comments are banned
(R13, M3).

Evidence IDs refer to `../../linkedin/references/evidence.md`. No vendor figure on how much more employee posts
reach than company posts has passed the evidence check; quote none.

## 1. Intake (the program lead)

Ask one at a time:
- the goal: hiring, pipeline, reputation in a field, a launch;
- who takes part, and whether each one volunteered (a program people are ordered into produces the same post
  many times);
- the company's rules: confidential topics, regulated claims (finance, health, legal), customers that may not be
  named, an existing social media policy;
- who reviews, and how fast they can turn a draft around.

Done when goal, participants, rules and reviewer are known.

## 2. Onboard each person

For each participant, on their own machine or with the lead running it with them present:
1. `linkedin-setup` Quick setup with their own samples, then a short Story bank interview on their work. The
   persona is theirs: their voice, their names allowed and banned, their approval level.
2. Agree their rhythm with them: what they can keep up with their workload. People differ; a uniform quota is a
   finding, not a plan.
3. A first post from their own story bank (`linkedin-post`), approved by them.

A ghostwriter or lead may draft for someone, but the person approves every text published under their name; the
member is responsible for what they post (R18).

Done when every participant has a saved persona and a first post they approved.

## 3. Review rules

What the company reviews, and only this:
- facts about the company, its products, customers and numbers;
- confidential or unreleased information;
- claims that carry legal or regulatory risk in their industry.

What it leaves to the person: voice, style, opinions about their own work, topic choice within the program's
goal, formatting. A review that rewrites voice turns every account into the company page.

Agree a turnaround the reviewer can keep; a post that waits days for approval loses its moment and the person
stops drafting.

## 4. Rhythm

- A short weekly idea exchange: internal wins, questions customers asked, things people learned. Each person
  picks what fits them; nobody is assigned a topic.
- Each person plans their own week with the weekly plan mode, sized to their hours.
- Engagement between colleagues is fine when it is real (a comment that adds something, per `linkedin-comment`).
  Scheduled "everyone likes at 9" and comment rotas are pods (R13, M3).
- Resharing a colleague's or the company's post goes with the person's own commentary (`linkedin-comment`,
  reshare mode), never as a bare repost wave.
- Two participants never message the same person about the same thing. Each persona's log only sees its own
  account, so keep a shared list of who is talking to whom (the team's CRM, if `linkedin-setup` recorded one).
- The company page reshares and adds context rather than originating everything; for Pages, LinkedIn suggests
  weekly posting (P7, grade C).

## 5. What to measure

Measure the goal, not volume:

| Goal | Measure |
|---|---|
| Hiring | applications or candidate conversations that name a participant's post or profile |
| Pipeline | conversations started from posts and comments (replies, inbound messages), meetings that followed |
| Reputation | invitations, inbound questions and mentions on the program's topic |
| Program health | who is still posting after the first month, and why the others stopped |

Per person, the review of last week mode reads their log. Impressions per post are context, not the target:
one number per post is noise, and the log's outcomes are closer to the goal. Ask people why they stopped before
adding reminders or quotas.

## 6. Set-up output

Give the lead one document:
- goal, participants, rules, reviewer and turnaround;
- onboarding status per person (persona saved, first post approved);
- the weekly rhythm (idea exchange, planning, review);
- the measures from section 5 and when they are looked at.

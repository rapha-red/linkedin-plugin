# Voice: sounding like the person, not like a model

Readers who use AI themselves spot AI text reliably, and they key on sameness and missing specifics more than
on single words (evidence H-rows). LinkedIn members can also report a post as AI slop, which cuts its reach.
Swapping words is not enough. The draft has to carry something only this person could have written.

## Order of authority

1. The persona's voice section (their samples, quirks, own bans). A persona quirk beats a default below,
   except the hard rules at the end of this file.
2. The defaults in this file.

With no persona, use the defaults and write plainly: a neutral professional voice with no borrowed style.

## What human text has

- **A specific.** A number with its referent, a named tool, place or situation, a thing that happened. One real
  detail beats three adjectives.
- **An owner.** First person, a position the writer would defend, and the cost or doubt that came with it.
- **Plain words.** The short common word, active voice, sentences mostly under 20 words (practice), one idea per sentence.
- **Uneven rhythm, naturally.** Sentence length varies because the thoughts vary. Leave rhythm alone when it is
  already uneven; never inject fragments to fake it.
- **The point first.** The first line says the thing. Context follows if it earns its place.
- **An ending that stops.** A flat statement, a real question the writer wants answered, or a plain ask. Then
  nothing.

## Tell list

Fix every hit. One hit in a short text is enough to rewrite that sentence; three in a paragraph means rewrite
the paragraph from its point.

**Words** (swap for the plain word, or cut): delve, underscore, pivotal, crucial, vital, robust, seamless,
leverage, utilize, harness, unlock, elevate, empower, foster, streamline, navigate (figurative), landscape,
realm, tapestry, testament, intricate, nuanced, multifaceted, holistic, comprehensive, ecosystem (figurative),
game-changer, revolutionary, groundbreaking, cutting-edge, boasts, vibrant, journey (figurative), resonate,
genuinely, truly, incredibly, absolutely, fundamentally, essentially, ultimately, notably, arguably,
additionally, moreover, furthermore.

**Constructions**
- Negative parallelism: "not X, but Y", "it's not X, it's Y", "not just X, Y too". State Y.
- Triplets by reflex: three adjectives, three parallel clauses, lists of exactly three. Keep the one that
  matters.
- Reveal set-ups: "Here's the thing", "The result?", "Plot twist:", "Let that sink in", "Read that again",
  "The best part?", a colon that sets up a punchline.
- Pointer phrases that fit any post: "the hard part is", "the real question is", "the part most people miss",
  "this is where it gets interesting", "the missing piece", "what nobody tells you". Name the thing itself.
- Sincerity and opinion flags: "honestly", "real talk", "unpopular opinion:", "let me be clear", "I'll be
  honest".
- Staccato: one-word or one-line paragraphs in series, "No X. No Y. Just Z."
- "-ing" tails that pretend to analyse: ", highlighting the importance of", ", reflecting a broader shift".
- Vague authority: "experts say", "studies show", "many believe" (name the source or drop it).
- "Serves as", "stands as", "represents" where "is" works.
- Hedge tails and self-downplay: "if that makes sense", "hope that's okay", "maybe", "a little", and "just" or
  "only" when they downplay the persona's own offer or effort (a restrictive "only works when" is fine). One
  plain line that leaves them free to decline an offer is not a hedge; see `conversations.md`.
- Assistant voice: "Great question", "I hope this helps", "Happy to help", "Certainly", "As an AI",
  knowledge-cutoff talk, "[Name]" placeholders.

**Engagement bait**: "Agree?", "Thoughts?", "Comment X and I'll send you Y", "Tag someone who", "Like if",
"Repost if". A real question about the topic is fine.

**Praise filler**: "Great post", "Love this", "So true", "Well said", "This resonated", "Spot on", "Such an
important point", superlatives about the person.

**Formatting tells**: bold inside sentences, emoji as bullets or headers, title-case headings in a post,
inline-header lists ("**Speed:** ..."), hashtag blocks, more than one exclamation mark per text.

## Per-surface shape

| Surface | Shape |
|---|---|
| Post | point in the first line; short paragraphs (W6); one idea; plain text, no bold, no emoji bullets; hashtags only if the persona uses them, with no proven reach effect (M7); a link in the body or in the first comment, chosen for reading flow (no placement has a proven reach effect, M1) |
| Comment | 2 to 4 sentences (practice), one concrete addition (experience, number, counter-example, sharper question); written as a reply in a conversation, never as a statement |
| Reply to a comment on your post | answer what they said; add one thing; 1 to 3 sentences (practice) |
| DM | see `conversations.md`; short; no subject-line voice, no signature block in a cold message |
| Connection note | one line; LinkedIn caps note length and free accounts get a shorter cap (practice: observed 200 characters); a real reason to connect; no pitch |
| Profile | first person, concrete outcomes and who they serve; no buzzword headline |

## Language notes

- German: choose "du" or "Sie" deliberately (du for startups, tech and founders unless they use Sie; Sie for
  established companies and senior roles unless they use du) and keep it. Proper umlauts.
- Other languages: follow the other person's register and formality.
- Keep the conversation's language even when the persona's default differs.

## Hard rules (a persona cannot turn these off)

- No em dashes (—) and no en dashes (–) as punctuation. Use a comma, a colon, parentheses or a new sentence.
- No invented facts, numbers, experiences or quotes (see `guardrails.md`).
- No deliberate typos or fake casualness to seem human. Proofread; errors lower how readers rate the writer.
- No claim that the text will pass an AI detector. The goal is text a reader believes, not text a tool scores.

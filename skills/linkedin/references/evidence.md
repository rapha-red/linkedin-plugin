# Evidence: what is known about writing and engaging on LinkedIn

Last verified 2026-09-30. Every row below passed an independent source check (URL, quote, numbers, grade, retractions).
Consult on demand: find the surface, read the row, cite the ID.

**Grades**
- **A**: LinkedIn official (help pages, policies, engineering blog, executive posts) or peer-reviewed research or statute.
- **B**: large dataset with a disclosed method, usually from a vendor (selling tools or services, so selection bias applies), or an official statement reported only by the press.
- **C**: practitioner opinion, anecdote, official claim with no method.
- A minus (A-) means peer-reviewed or official but only partly verified at the source, or a preprint. "Transfer" means the study ran outside LinkedIn (email, lab, face-to-face) and applies by analogy.

**Rule for skills:** state the rule and cite the ID (for example "short first DMs get more replies (D2)"). Do not quote a number unless you also give its source. Never present a C row or a myth as fact.

Surfaces: `W` writing (all), `P` posts, `C` comments and replies, `D` DMs and inbox, `H` sounding human, `R` LinkedIn rules and platform, `L` legal, `M` myths, `G` gaps.

## W: Writing, all surfaces

| ID | Rule | Grade | Source | Caveat |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| W1 | Use the short common word; needlessly complex words make the writer look less intelligent. | A | Oppenheimer, Applied Cognitive Psychology, doi:10.1002/acp.1178, 2006 | Lab texts, not LinkedIn. |
| W2 | Keep every message as short as its job allows; shorter asks tend to get more responses. | A (transfer) | Cochrane review Edwards et al., doi:10.1002/14651858.MR000008.pub5, 2023 (brief vs detailed letter OR 3.26); Rogers and Lasky-Fink, https://behavioralscientist.org/when-writing-for-busy-readers-less-is-more/, 2023 | Survey and email evidence. Rogers and Lasky-Fink (49 vs 127 words, 4.8% vs 2.7% response) is a book-reported field test, grade C/B. See D2 for LinkedIn data. |
| W3 | Aim a cold message at about 50-125 words, a simple reading level and one to three questions. | B | Boomerang, https://blog.boomerangapp.com/2016/02/7-tips-for-getting-more-responses-to-your-emails-with-data/, 2016 | Email vendor, over 40M emails from users who set reminders (selection bias), correlational. 3rd-grade level 53% vs college 39%; 8+ questions did 20% worse than 3. Under 50 words did no better than very long mails, so W2 has a floor. |
| W4 | Name the specific thing, number or example instead of the category. | A (transfer) | Packard and Berger, J. Consumer Research, https://academic.oup.com/jcr/article/47/5/787/6294178, 2020 | Customer-service calls and emails. The +8.9% satisfaction per SD of concreteness is from the call study only. |
| W5 | Use a precise number only when you really have it; readers take precision as a sign of the speaker's confidence. | A- | Jerez-Fernandez, Angulo, Oppenheimer, Psychological Science, https://www.psychologicalscience.org/news/were-only-human/precisely-right-no-doubt-trust-me.html, 2014 | Two short studies; about perceived confidence, not persuasion. Too much precision can backfire (Loschelder et al. 2016). |
| W6 | Write for scanners: concise, short paragraphs, no promotional tone. | B | Nielsen Norman Group, https://www.nngroup.com/articles/concise-scannable-and-objective-how-to-write-for-the-web/ and https://www.nngroup.com/articles/how-users-read-on-the-web/, 1997 | Web pages, n=51. "Point first" was not tested. |
| W7 | Use active voice and address the reader as "you". | B | digital.gov plain-language guide, https://digital.gov/guides/plain-language/writing/, current | A federal standard, not an experiment. |
| W8 | Keep a first professional contact free of smiley emoji; they lower perceived competence. | A | Glikson, Cheshin, van Kleef, SPPS, https://pure.uva.nl/ws/files/30232658/1948550617720269.pdf, 2018 | 3 experiments (N=90, 203, 85). | W9 | Proofread; a clean text is rated higher than one with typos. | A | Boland and Queen, PLoS ONE, doi:10.1371/journal.pone.0149885, 2016 | n=83, MTurk, housemate-ad emails. |
| W10 | State tone and intent plainly; sarcasm and subtle tone do not survive text. | A | Kruger, Epley, Parker, Ng, JPSP, doi:10.1037/0022-3514.89.6.925, 2005 | Email, 5 experiments. |

## D: DMs and inbox (openers, steering, follow-ups, exits, no)

No peer-reviewed study of LinkedIn DMs exists (G2). Rows are LinkedIn's own data, vendor data, or transfer.

| ID | Rule | Grade | Source | Caveat |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| D1 | Write each first message for one person, not from a bulk template. | A (transfer) | LinkedIn InMail data, https://www.linkedin.com/business/talent/blog/talent-strategy/these-inmails-get-best-response-rates, 2022 (individually sent about +15%); Sahni, Wheeler, Chintagunta, https://ideas.repec.org/a/inm/ormksc/v37y2018i2p236-258.html, 2018; Cochrane Edwards 2023 (personalised OR 1.24) | Sahni tested only the name in a subject line. Also Short 2015 (doi:10.1186/s12874-015-0063-5, quasi-randomised, 1.5x) and Cureton 2021 (doi:10.1186/s13063-021-05452-w, weak effect). |
| D2 | Keep a first DM short: a few sentences, not a page. | A- | LinkedIn InMail data (link in D1), 2022; Expandi, https://expandi.io/state-of-linkedin-outreach-h2-2026/, 2026 | InMails under 400 characters +22%, over 1,200 -11% (corporate recruiters, 30-day window, correlational). Expandi reply peak at 151-200 characters is B (automation vendor). |
| D3 | Open on a real, uncommon commonality (same niche, same problem); a common one does nothing. | A (transfer) | Burger et al., PSPB, doi:10.1177/0146167203258838, 2004 | Small student samples; "common does nothing" is from Study 4. LinkedIn's "5x more likely to engage with someone who shares mutual connections" has no method (C). |
| D4 | Asking for someone's advice is a legitimate opener; it reads as competent. | B | Brooks, Gino, Schweitzer, Management Science, doi:10.1287/mnsc.2014.2054, 2015 | Gino ran at least one study; no raw data exists. Do not rely on it alone. |
| D5 | Give the reason for the ask ("because...") and keep the ask small. | A- | Langer, Blank, Chanowitz, doi:10.1037/0022-3514.36.6.635, 1978; Burger review, doi:10.1207/s15327957pspr0304_2, 1999 | The famous 60/94% figures are secondary only. Foot-in-the-door has moderators and sometimes fails. |
| D6 | End an ask with a genuine out ("totally fine if not"). | A | Carpenter meta-analysis (42 studies), doi:10.1080/10510974.2012.727941, 2013 | Weaker when the decision is not immediate. Transfer. |
| D7 | In a cold first message, ask for interest ("worth a look?"); ask for a time slot once they reply. | B | Gong, https://www.gong.io/resources/labs/this-surprising-cold-email-cta-will-help-you-book-a-lot-more-meetings/, date not given | 304,174 emails, vendor, email not LinkedIn. |
| D8 | Just ask; people say yes and appreciate outreach more than you predict. | A | Flynn and Lake, JPSP, doi:10.1037/0022-3514.95.1.128, 2008; Liu et al., JPSP, doi:10.1037/pspi0000402, 2023 | "Up to 50%" underestimate is an upper bound. |
| D9 | Reach beyond close contacts; moderately weak ties are the most valuable. | A | Rajkumar et al., Science, https://digitaleconomy.stanford.edu/publication/a-causal-test-of-the-strength-of-weak-ties, 2022 | LinkedIn randomised test on over 20M members, job mobility; varies by industry. |
| D10 | Converse: take turns sharing, ask follow-ups, and move a real exchange to a live conversation. | A- (transfer) | Sprecher et al., JESP, doi:10.1016/j.jesp.2013.03.017, 2013; Roghanizad and Bohns, JESP, doi:10.1016/j.jesp.2016.10.002, 2017 | Lab get-acquainted task; in-person asks beat email (about 70% vs 10%, students). | D11 | Follow up: two to four follow-ups (three to five messages in total), then stop. | B | Belkins, https://belkins.io/blog/sales-follow-up-statistics, 2025; Expandi (link in D2), 2026 | Email: steps 2-6 bring 58.6% of replies. LinkedIn: reply rate peaks at 3 messages (9.8%), falls to 5.0% at 5+. Vendors; stop at any no (L3). |
| D12 | After a no to a request, one polite follow-up is defensible; never repeat the pitch, and "not interested" ends it. | A (transfer) | Newark, Flynn, Bohns, SPPS, doi:10.1177/1948550613490967, 2014; L3 | Studies used a similar-size second request; people agree partly from discomfort at refusing twice, so this is a pressure risk. |
| D13 | Offer the exit yourself ("happy to leave it here"). | A (transfer) | Mastroianni et al., PNAS, doi:10.1073/pnas.2011809118, 2021 | 932 live conversations; "offer the exit" is our inference. |
| D14 | Follow up after a good exchange; they liked you more than you think. | A (transfer) | Boothby et al., Psychological Science, doi:10.1177/0956797618783714, 2018 | Live conversation. |
| D15 | Meet an objection with a pause and a clarifying question before answering. | B | Gong, https://www.gong.io/resources/labs/handling-sales-objections/, date not given | 67,149 recorded sales calls, vendor; C3 is the peer-reviewed analogue. |
| D16 | A brief apology for a circumstance you did not cause ("I know it's end of week") builds trust; do not apologise for the message itself. | A (transfer) | Brooks, Dai, Schweitzer, SPPS, doi:10.1177/1948550613506122, 2014 | Weak transfer. |
| D17 | Add a connection note when you want replies; expect slightly lower acceptance. | B | Belkins, https://belkins.io/blog/linkedin-outreach-study, 2025 | 14,077 contacts: note 25.3% accept / 8.2% reply, no note 27.6% / 5.3%. | D18 | Write your own words; AI-hyperpersonalised messages did not beat human-written ones. | B | Expandi (link in D2), 2026; Belkins (link in D17), 2025 | Expandi: about 12% lower acceptance (unit unclear), reply flat; Belkins: 7.3% vs 7.0% reply. Vendor data. |
| D19 | Do not optimise the weekday for DMs; it barely matters. | B | Expandi, Belkins (links above); LinkedIn InMail data (D1) | Weekday spread under 1 point; InMail Monday best "but just barely". Expandi saw morning acceptance above evening (about 32% vs 24%). |

## H: Sounding human and AI perception

| ID | Rule | Grade | Source | Caveat |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| H1 | Avoid the words that spiked with LLM use ("delve", "showcasing", "underscores"). | A | Kobak et al., Science Advances, https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.07016, 2025 | Over 15M PubMed abstracts; at least 13.5% of 2024 abstracts LLM-processed, up to 40% in some subcorpora. |
| H2 | Assume your readers meet AI text daily and know its tells. | A | Liang et al., Patterns, https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.09747, 2025 | Late 2024: about 18% of financial complaints, 24% of corporate press releases. |
| H3 | Remove the known AI tells: puffery, vague attributions, "not just X, but Y", rule of three, sycophantic chat voice ("Great question!"), emoji as formatting, excess bold. They are signs, not proof. | B | Wikipedia, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Signs_of_AI_writing, living | Editor consensus, not an experiment. The page also lists ineffective indicators; do not treat those as tells. |
| H4 | Do not rely on "human-sounding" tricks (first person, family mentions); readers use them but they are uninformative. | A | Jakesch, Hancock, Naaman, PNAS, https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10089155, 2023 | n=4,600: detection near chance. "More human than human" applied only to text optimised against those heuristics. |
| H5 | Make the substance original and specific; frequent AI users spot AI text even after tell-words are removed. | A | Russell, Karpinska, Iyyer, ACL, https://aclanthology.org/2025.acl-long.267, 2025 | 5-expert majority misclassified 1 of 300 articles. That the GTM/tech audience is such a population is our inference. |
| H6 | Keep the substance yours so there is nothing to expose; disclosing AI use lowers trust, and being exposed is worse. | A | Schilke and Reimann, OBHDP, https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/jobhdp/v188y2025ics0749597825000172.html, 2025 | 13 experiments; d about 0.8 in Study 1; smaller but present for tech-positive readers. |
| H7 | Write replies yourself when the relationship matters; people suspected of AI replies are rated more negatively. | A | Hohenstein et al., Scientific Reports, https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10073210, 2023 | Actual smart-reply use made partners seem closer; suspicion is the cost. |
| H8 | Treat a profile or bio as trust-critical; text believed AI-written is trusted less. | A | Jakesch, French, Ma, Hancock, CHI, doi:10.1145/3290605.3300469, 2019 | Airbnb profiles in a mixed pool. |
| H9 | For personal messages, own the content; AI-attributed messages read as less authentic and disclosure does not fix it. | A | Glikson and Asscher, Computers in Human Behavior, https://cris.bgu.ac.il/en/publications/ai-mediated-apology-in-a-multilingual-work-context-implications-f/, 2023 | Apologies at work; light AI use (1 of 3 tools) had no cost. Nakano et al. preprint (arXiv:2510.24011) agrees; AI literacy softens it. |
| H10 | Add your own angle before posting; AI help makes texts converge on each other and on a Western-generic style. | A | Doshi and Hauser, Science Advances, https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11244532, 2024; Agarwal, Naaman, Vashistha, CHI, https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.11360, 2025 | Story-writing and lab tasks. |
| H11 | Judge drafts by reading them, not with AI detectors; detectors are unreliable. | A | https://searchengineland.com/openai-ai-classifier-no-longer-available-429912, 2023; Liang et al., Patterns, https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.02819, 2023 | OpenAI withdrew its classifier for low accuracy; seven detectors flagged 61.3% of non-native essays. |
| H12 | Fix substance, not surface; paraphrase "humanizers" target detectors, not readers. | A- | Cheng, Sadasivan et al., https://arxiv.org/html/2506.07001, 2025 | Preprint; detector metrics only (about 88% drop), plain paraphrasing made some detectors better. "Not readers" is inference from H5. |

## M: Myths

| ID | Claim | Verdict | What is actually known |
|---|---|---|---|
| M1 | "Links in the post kill reach (-60%)" | unproven | No LinkedIn statement. Datasets disagree: van der Blom 2026 about -18.8% median reach; Ordinal (tryordinal.com/blog/linkedin-link-penalty-study) -26.5% average, carried by company pages, near zero on personal profiles; Metricool 2025 +13.6% interactions. ## Style decisions (not evidence)

- **No em dashes.** Readers associate them with AI text (M25). This is a style choice, not a claim that em dashes are detected or penalised.
- **No pitch in a DM before the other person replies.** Our default. Nothing tests it directly (M17); the legal caution is in L1-L4. A user can allow a first-message offer (`pitch_before_reply: on`).

# Interview questions

Questions that get material a draft can use, grouped by the persona section they fill. The dividing line: a
useful question asks about one moment ("when did that last happen?"); a useless one asks about a category ("what
are your strengths?") and gets a summary back, which no post can use.

Ask one per message, in your own phrasing, in the user's language. Adapt; this is a bank, not a script.

## Essentials (Quick setup)

- Who: "In one or two sentences, what do you do and for whom?" Then, if it came back as a title: "And what does a
  normal week of that actually look like?"
- Audience: "Who do you want reading your posts? Think of two or three real people you would like to reach."
- Offer: "What do you hope LinkedIn brings you: clients, hires, a new role, a reputation for something?" Then:
  "When is it fair to mention that in a post or message?" Then: "Which products or companies are you tied to
  (employer, your own product, a client)? Any mention of them will say so."
- Language: "Which language do you mostly post in? Anything I should know about how formal you are?"

## Timeline

- "What were you hired to do there, and what did you end up doing?"
- "What changed between the day you joined and the day you left?"
- On "recently" or "a while ago": "Which month was that, roughly?"

## Results

The section drafts need most and users fill least. Every number needs what it measures and over what period.

- "Put a number on it, even a rough one. I'll note that it's rough."
- "What was it before, and what was it after?"
- "How long did that take, and how many people worked on it?"
- "How would you show it to someone who doubted you?"

## Built, shipped, changed

- "What exists now that would not exist without you?"
- "Which part of it was yours, and which was the team's?" (keeps posts from claiming a team's work)
- "What do people point at when they describe your work?"

## Turning points and mistakes

- "What did you believe a year ago that you no longer believe? What changed your mind?"
- "What is the most expensive mistake you have made at work?"
- "What do you check now that you never used to check?" (reaches a mistake without asking for a confession)

Ask once here, never press twice.

## Positions

- "What do you think is true in your field that most people around you disagree with?"
- "What does holding that view cost you?" A view with no cost is an opinion everyone shares; keep looking.
- "Who would argue with you about this, and what would they say?"

## Stories they already tell

- "Which two or three stories do you end up telling over dinner or in meetings?"
- "What do people ask you about more than once?"

## Names and limits

Ask directly, for each name or number that came up.

- "Can I name <company> in a post, keep it anonymous, or ask you each time?"
- "Is anything here under NDA, in negotiation, or not public yet?"
- "Any topics you want kept out of your posts entirely?"

## Pressing a vague answer (once)

| They said | Ask once |
|---|---|
| "we improved it a lot" | "By how much, measured how, over what time?" |
| "a big client" | "Can I name them, or should it stay anonymous?" |
| "recently" | "Which month?" |
| "it was hard" | "What was the moment it was hardest?" |
| "I learned a lot" | "What do you do differently now because of it?" |

Whatever the second answer is, record it and move on.

## Questions that waste the session

| Question | What comes back |
|---|---|
| "Tell me about yourself" | a CV read aloud |
| "What are your strengths?" / "What makes you unique?" | adjectives and hedging |
| "What are your content pillars?" | marketing words; it hands them the strategy work |
| "Describe a challenge you overcame" | a rehearsed job-interview answer |
| several questions in one message | an answer to the last one only |

## Hard moments

- **They go quiet.** Usually they are remembering. Wait; if it stays quiet, make it concrete: "What did you
  work on on Tuesday?"
- **They talk for a long time.** Let them. The usable detail is often in the tangent; follow it.
- **They decline.** Record the topic under Off limits, say you have, and change subject.

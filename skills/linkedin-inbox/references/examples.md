# Inbox shapes: triage table and replies

Shapes, not templates. People and details are invented; the reasoning is what carries over.

## A triage table

| # | Person | Last from | Waiting | Rung | Lang | Action | Why |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Maria K., ops lead, logistics | them | 2 d | 2 | EN | reply now | asked what the user's clients do about night-shift training |

Order: reply now (oldest waiting first), close, follow up due, leave.

## Answer first

Their message: "Interesting. What do other warehouses do about training the night shift?"

Weak:
> Great question! Training is a crucial part of any rollout. There are many approaches, and every warehouse is
> different. Would you be open to a call to discuss your specific situation?

Why weak: assistant voice, filler, no answer, a call ask at rung 2.

Good:
> The ones I've seen do best pair each new scanner user with a day-shift picker for two shifts before they go
> solo. Is your night shift mostly new hires, or people moving over from days?

Why good: the answer in the first line, from the persona's story bank; then one question on what she said (C2).
Every detail must be one the persona actually has; with none, ask the user for it before drafting.

## Offer only when earned

Their message: "We've tried two tools for this and both were a pain to set up. What do you use?"

That line earns rung 3: they named the problem and asked. The card quotes it.

Good:
> We use our own, mostly because setup was the pain for us too: it runs off your existing pick lists, so there
> is nothing to re-enter. Here's the overview: <link>. No worries if the timing is off.

Why good: their words for the problem, the offer as a fact, one link, one plain out (D6).

The same offer after "Busy week, all good here" would be unearned: stay on their topic instead.

## Objection

Their message: "If this is a sales thing, I'm not interested."

Weak:
> I completely understand! I'm not trying to sell you anything, I just thought you might find value in what we
> do. Let me explain.

Good:
> Fair enough. I do work on this, but I wrote because of your post on the scanner rollout. Did the error
> numbers hold after the first month?

Why good: an honest acknowledgement in a few words (the user does work in this field; the reason for writing
was their post), then their topic with something real. If they asked a real question
inside the objection, a clarifying question comes before any defence (D15).

## Polite no

Their message: "Thanks, but not something we're looking at this year."

Good:
> All good, thanks for saying so.

Then log `outcome: no` and a 90-day `suppress`. Nothing more, and no ambient engagement pitched at them in the
meantime.

## Language switch

The user opened in English; Ines answered in German with "Sie". The reply is written natively in German, keeps
"Sie", and the card says `language: DE (switched from EN)`. It stays German unless she switches back.

## Handoff

Their message: "Happy to talk. When works for you?"

The agent asks the user: "Which days and times work, and in which time zone? Or a booking link?" The user
answers "Tue or Wed, 2 to 5 pm, Berlin time".

Good:
> Tuesday or Wednesday afternoon works for me, anywhere from 2 to 5 pm Berlin time. Which suits you?

Why good: every slot came from the user in this session.

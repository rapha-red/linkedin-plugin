# Profile sections: checks and rewrite shapes

Each section: what it is for, the checks (each one a possible finding), and the shape of a rewrite. No field
limits are listed here because LinkedIn changes them; the edit form shows the current counter.

Evidence IDs refer to `../../linkedin/references/evidence.md`. A check without an ID is practice: say so if the
user asks why.

## Headline

For: the line shown next to the name everywhere (search, comments, invitations, messages). Most visitors read
nothing else.

Checks:
- Says what the person does and for whom, in words the audience would use to search or describe it (R2).
- Carries one concrete proof or differentiator from the story bank, if one exists (W4).
- A title alone ("Founder & CEO", "Senior Engineer") says nothing about who they help. `high` when the goal is
  clients or hiring.
- Buzzword stacks, vague adjectives ("passionate", "driven", "results-oriented"), emoji as decoration, all caps
  (W1, W8).
- A claim the story bank cannot back.

Shape: `<what you do> for <who>` plus, optionally, `| <proof or current role>`. Two options: one plain, one that
leads with the proof. Keep it readable when cut short after the first few words, since many surfaces truncate it.

## About

For: the reason to reply, connect or book. Readers see the opening lines before expanding (P9 applies by
analogy: the point goes where the reader decides to stay).

Checks:
- The first line says the point (who they help with what), not a greeting, a life story or a question for effect.
- First person. Third person ("Jane is a seasoned...") reads as a bio someone else wrote.
- At least one specific: a result with what it measures, a named thing they built, a situation they know well
  (W4, W5).
- Short paragraphs a phone can scan (W6).
- Ends with one next step that fits the goal (a way to reach them, what to send, where to see work), or a flat
  last line. "Let's connect!" is not a next step.
- Keywords for what they want to be found for appear naturally in sentences, not as a keyword dump (R2).
- AI tells from `../../linkedin/references/voice.md`; a profile is trust-critical, so fix every hit (H8).

Shape, in this order and only as long as the material allows:
1. What you do and for whom, in one or two sentences.
2. How you do it or what makes your approach yours: a position from the story bank.
3. One or two proof points from the story bank.
4. What you are working on now, if relevant to the goal.
5. One next step.

## Experience

For: proof that the headline is true. Also read by the feed as part of the author's identity (R3).

Checks:
- Current role is present, titled as the audience would understand it, and matches the headline.
- Each role says what they owned and what changed because of them, not a list of duties ("responsible for").
- Results carry numbers only where the story bank has them, with the referent (W5).
- Team results are credited as team results (the story bank records which part was theirs).
- Old or irrelevant roles are short; recent relevant roles carry the detail.
- Media or links attached where they prove something (a shipped product, a talk, a case study).

Shape per role: one line on the scope (team, market, what they were hired to do), then two to four lines each
naming a thing done and its effect. Past tense for past roles.

## Featured

For: the one place the user picks what a visitor sees first.

Checks:
- Each item supports the goal from step 1 (clients: proof of results and a way to start; hiring: what it is like
  to work there and open roles; job search: best work samples; reputation: best posts, talks, writing).
- Few items, each with a title that says what the visitor gets.
- Nothing stale or off-topic (an old event, a reshared corporate post that is not theirs).
- Empty: a finding only when there is something worth featuring; otherwise suggest what to create later.

Shape: an ordered list of 1 to 3 items with a title per item and why it is there.

## Skills

For: search and the feed's identity signal (R3).

Checks:
- The skills listed first are the ones they want to be found for, and match headline and experience.
- Skills they no longer practise or want to be hired for are removed or moved down.
- Generic soft skills with no role backing them add nothing.

Shape: a proposed top group and a list to remove, each with one line of reason.

## Photo and banner

Guidance only; you cannot see a photo unless the user shares it.

Photo checks: a recent photo that looks like them on a video call, face clearly visible, no group crop, no
sunglasses. A profile without a photo reads as inactive or fake (practice).

Banner checks: says something the headline does not (what they do, a proof, a product, a cause), or is a calm
image that does not compete with the photo. Text sits where the profile photo does not cover it; check on a
phone. Not a default or random stock image.

## Custom URL

Check: a readable address (their name, or name plus a short word) rather than the default string of letters and
numbers. Set in "Edit public profile & URL". Practice; useful in signatures and when said aloud.

## Recommendations

Checks: a few recent recommendations from people who saw the work the headline claims (clients, managers,
colleagues), each specific about a project or result. Old or generic ones add little.

To help: list two or three people from the story bank who could speak to the goal, and offer to draft each
request as a short personal message (hand-off `linkedin-dm`, warm network opener). A request names the project to
write about and offers to write one back.
